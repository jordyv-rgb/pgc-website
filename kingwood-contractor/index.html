Why Kingwood

Homes built in the 1970s to 1990s are reaching the right age for quality improvement work.

Kitchens from the 1980s and 90s

Cabinet boxes built 30–40 years ago are still structurally sound, but layouts, hardware, countertop materials and finishes reflect a different era. A kitchen remodel in a Kingwood home isn't starting from scratch — it's bringing a well-built room forward with updated materials and a clearer scope.

Original bathrooms and shower surrounds

Fiberglass shower pans, one-piece surrounds and tile from the 1990s are at or past the point where they can be patched. A proper shower remodel — substrate, waterproofing, new tile — handles the problem correctly instead of masking it for another few years.

Flooring through multiple families

Many Kingwood homes have seen two or three owners. The carpet is long overdue and the original tile or laminate reflects decades of use. Waterproof LVP and tile flooring are the right answer — durable, moisture-resistant and appropriate for the scale and style of these homes.

Roofs approaching end of service life

Asphalt shingle roofs typically have a 20–30 year service life. Homes built in the late 1980s and 1990s are right at or past that window. Add in Houston's weather history and many Kingwood roofs need a serious look — not just a patch.

Structural and foundation work

Houston's soil and climate conditions create foundation movement in homes across this age range. We identify, scope and execute structural repairs with the same organized contractor approach we bring to finish work — because foundation issues caught early are significantly less expensive to address.

Interior work: paint, sheetrock, carpentry

Interior trim, sheetrock repairs, painted surfaces and custom carpentry details age alongside the home. These are the improvements that make a house feel current and cared for — and they're often what homeowners notice most after everything else is done.

03 — How We Work

A clear, phased process — and a payment structure that follows the work.

Every Kingwood project follows the same organized process. You see exactly what is happening, when it's happening, and what is being paid for at each step. Payments via Zelle, ACH, or Stripe — never a large deposit before a single board is touched.

— Phase 01

On-Site Consultation

We come to your Kingwood home, walk the space, photograph existing conditions and talk through what the project actually involves — before any scope is written or money changes hands.

— Phase 02

Written Scope & Deposit

A clear written proposal — what's included, what's not, material direction and timeline. A modest deposit reserves your project window. Nothing starts without a document you've read and agreed to.

Build Phase

Work begins. Materials arrive, crews show up on schedule, progress payments are tied to milestones — not to calendar dates. You always know what stage the project is in.

We walk the completed work with you, close out the punch list, clean up the site and hand the home back in better condition than we found it. Final payment follows the walk — not a timer.
